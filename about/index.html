06 / ABOUT ME

From rural Argentina to computational social science.

Fulbright Scholar · International Relations · Applied Quantitative Methods

I am a Fulbright Scholar pursuing a Master’s in Applied Quantitative Methods and Social Analysis at Northeastern University.

I grew up in rural Argentina on an ecological farm powered by solar energy, where my family ran a community-based educational project. At 18, I moved to Buenos Aires to pursue higher education. A merit-based scholarship led me to the ESPOP public opinion survey and to quantitative research.

At ZIGLA, I worked on the evaluation of social programs, including IDB Lab initiatives, developing monitoring frameworks and quantitative indicators. I later joined Control Arms, where my work focused on transparency and compliance in international security governance.

I independently designed and developed the Arms Transfers Dashboard to make complex global reporting data accessible to governments, researchers, and civil society. I presented its analytical framework at the Arms Trade Treaty Working Group on Transparency and Reporting and later became the youngest member of the ATT Monitor Steering Committee.

Across these experiences, my focus has remained consistent: using quantitative and computational methods to examine international security, transparency, public opinion, and human rights.
