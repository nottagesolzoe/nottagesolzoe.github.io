06 / ABOUT ME

From rural Argentina to computational social science.

Computational methods for more people. Theory, practice and community.

My work aims to make computational methods accessible to a wider public. I want to make tools easier to understand, use visualization to communicate questions and findings, and bridge theory and practice. Research, teaching and reusable resources are different ways of working toward that purpose.

I have lived in Rosario. From the age of 12 to 18, I lived in the rural area of Arroyo Seco, where my family still lives. My parents ran a community-based educational project on an ecological farm powered by solar energy. That experience shaped me: from an early age, it taught me to want to give back to my community and help others access the opportunities I had.

At 18, I moved to Buenos Aires to study International Relations at Universidad de San Andrés, supported by a scholarship and educational financing. The ESPOP public opinion survey introduced me to quantitative research. Through that experience, I found a way to connect social science questions with data, methods and tools that could be used beyond the classroom.

At ZIGLA, I worked on social program evaluation, developing monitoring frameworks, indicators, surveys and dashboards. Later, at Control Arms, I focused on arms-transfer transparency. I designed and developed the Arms Transfers Dashboard to make complex information more accessible to governments, researchers and civil society organizations. Presenting it and teaching people how to use it put that connection between research and practice into action.

The Fulbright scholarship opened a new chapter in Boston, where I am pursuing a Master’s in Applied Quantitative Methods and Social Analysis at Northeastern University. There, I am deepening my training in computational social science while contributing to research and teaching. This opportunity also strengthens my commitment to sharing what I learn and helping others find their own paths.

This portfolio brings together projects, visualizations, teaching materials and reusable tools. I want them to help people explore a question, understand a method and feel ready to apply it. Making things simpler without losing rigor is part of how I understand my work and my relationship with the community.
