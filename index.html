/ ABOUT ME

I study how evidence
can make power visible.

I am an international-relations researcher working across international security, transparency, public opinion, and human rights. I use computational methods to find structure in complex information—and to make that information easier for other people to question, understand, and use.

01

/ ROOTS · RURAL ARGENTINA

Public purpose
came first.

I grew up on an ecological farm powered by solar energy, where my family ran a community-based educational project. Living close to both the land and a collective learning space shaped my commitment to public service, inclusion, and the idea that knowledge carries a responsibility beyond the individual.

ROOTS

02 / BUENOS AIRES · AGE 18

A scholarship.
A first dataset.

At eighteen, I moved to Buenos Aires to pursue higher education—my first major life transition. A merit-based scholarship opened the door to Universidad de San Andrés and to the ESPOP Political Satisfaction and Public Opinion Survey.

Working with survey data introduced me to quantitative research. It also made visible the technical spaces in which women were still underrepresented, strengthening my determination to build a career in them.

03 / EVALUATION → INTERNATIONAL SECURITY

Learning what
evidence must do.

At ZIGLA, I worked on evaluations of social programs across Mexico, Uruguay, the United Kingdom, and the United States, including initiatives supported by IDB Lab. I learned to translate broad social goals into monitoring frameworks, indicators, and evidence that institutions could act on.

I then moved into the security field through Control Arms and the ATT Monitor, applying that same discipline to transparency, compliance, and accountability in international arms governance.

04 / MAKING COMPLEX DATA USABLE

The dashboard
entered the room.

As an ATT Monitor Research Associate, I designed and developed an interactive Power BI dashboard from more than 25,000 fields of reported arms-transfer information. The goal was not visualization for its own sake: it was to make fragmented reporting usable for governments, researchers, journalists, and civil society.

I presented the dashboard at CSP10 and the CSP11 Working Group on Transparency and Reporting. I later became the youngest member of the ATT Monitor Steering Committee, contributing to strategic oversight and editorial direction.

05 / NOW · BOSTON

New methods.
The same question.

As a Fulbright Scholar at Northeastern University, I am deepening my work in computational social science through statistical modeling, machine learning, text analysis, and network methods.

Across UN voting networks, news corpora, public-opinion data, and arms-transfer reporting, the question remains consistent: how can complex evidence reveal relationships that matter—and become clear enough to support better research and more accountable institutions?
